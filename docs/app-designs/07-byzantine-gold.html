The principle of Sola Scriptura holds that Scripture alone is the final authority on matters of faith and practice. As Paul writes in 2 Timothy 3:16-17, "All Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness, that the man of God may be complete, equipped for every good work."
SP
SisterPatricia Side B Silver Patristic 2 days ago
"...the man of God may be complete, equipped for every good work."
This verse speaks to Scripture's sufficiency for equipping, but does not preclude the role of authoritative Tradition. The early Church Fathers relied on oral apostolic tradition alongside the written texts. Irenaeus explicitly appeals to the "rule of faith" passed down through bishops.
TM
TheologianMark Side A Gold Historical 2 days ago
While the Fathers valued tradition, they consistently appealed to Scripture as the ultimate arbiter. Athanasius, in his defense against the Arians, argued from Scripture, not from tradition alone. The question is whether tradition has equal authority or serves as a subordinate witness.
DL
DeaconLuke Neutral Bronze Philosophical 1 day ago
I think both sides raise valid concerns. The real question might be epistemological: how do we adjudicate between competing interpretations of Scripture without some form of interpretive authority? This seems to be the crux of the disagreement rather than Scripture's sufficiency per se.
