Is Sola Scriptura a Valid Doctrine?

Examining whether Scripture alone is sufficient as the sole rule of faith and practice, or if Sacred Tradition and Church authority are also necessary.

RP
ReformedPastor Sola Scriptura Trusted 2 days ago
The sufficiency of Scripture is affirmed in 2 Timothy 3:16-17: "All Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness, that the man of God may be complete, equipped for every good work." The key word here is "complete" -- if Scripture equips us for every good work, what else could be necessary? The early Reformers recognized this principle not as an innovation, but as a return to apostolic Christianity.

CS
CatholicScholar Scripture + Tradition Established 2 days ago
This passage says Scripture is "profitable," not "sufficient alone." There is a significant theological difference. Paul is writing to Timothy, who already received oral teaching -- the very Tradition the Catholic Church upholds. Furthermore, 2 Thessalonians 2:15 explicitly commands: "Stand firm and hold to the traditions which you were taught by us, either by word of mouth or by our letter." Scripture itself points beyond itself to authoritative Tradition.

HB
HistoryBuff Neutral Leans: Side A 1 day ago
Looking at this historically, the canon itself wasn't settled until the councils of Hippo (393) and Carthage (397). This creates an interesting paradox for the strict Sola Scriptura position: the very collection of books considered "Scripture" was determined by the Church's authoritative councils. However, one could argue that the councils merely recognized what was already inherently authoritative, rather than conferring authority upon those texts. Both sides have strong historical arguments here.

EO
EasternOrthodox Scripture + Tradition 18 hours ago
The Orthodox perspective adds another dimension here. We don't separate Scripture and Tradition as two competing sources the way this debate often frames it. For us, Scripture is Tradition -- it is the written witness of the living Tradition of the Church, which also includes liturgical practice, conciliar decisions, and the consensus of the Fathers. The question "Scripture alone or Scripture plus Tradition?" is itself a Western framing that the Eastern Church would reframe entirely.
