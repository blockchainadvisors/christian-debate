TM
TheologianMark Side A Gold Historical Argument 3 days ago

Scripture itself claims sufficiency for doctrine and practice. 2 Timothy 3:16-17 states that all Scripture is God-breathed and is useful for teaching, rebuking, correcting, and training in righteousness, so that the servant of God may be thoroughly equipped for every good work. The phrase "thoroughly equipped" suggests no additional source of authority is necessary.

SR
SisterRachel Side B Silver Scriptural Rebuttal 2 days ago

But 2 Timothy 3:16-17 doesn't say Scripture is the only authority -- it says it's useful. Paul also writes in 2 Thessalonians 2:15, "hold to the traditions which you were taught by us, either by word of mouth or by letter." This clearly acknowledges oral tradition as authoritative alongside written Scripture.

PJ
PastorJames Side A Gold Counter-Argument 1 day ago

The "traditions" Paul refers to in 2 Thessalonians 2:15 were the apostolic teachings that later became Scripture. There's no evidence of a separate, ongoing oral tradition that contradicts or supplements what was eventually written down. The canon was the fulfillment of those traditions.

DL
DeaconLuke Neutral Bronze Clarifying Question 12 hours ago

I find both arguments compelling. Could either side clarify how the early church fathers (pre-canon) determined doctrine? If they relied on oral tradition, does that invalidate Sola Scriptura, or was that a transitional period before the canon was finalized?
