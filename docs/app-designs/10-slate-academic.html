Is Sola Scriptura a Valid Doctrine?

TheologianMark Side A Tier III — Established 3 days ago
The doctrine of Sola Scriptura holds that Scripture alone is the final authority for Christian faith and practice. As articulated during the Reformation, this principle asserts that all necessary doctrine is contained within the biblical canon.1 The early church fathers, including Athanasius and Cyril of Jerusalem, repeatedly appealed to Scripture as the supreme standard against which all teaching must be measured.2

PatristicScholar Side B Tier IV — Trusted 3 days ago
With respect, this reading of the Church Fathers is selective. Athanasius also affirmed the authority of ecumenical councils to define orthodox teaching. Basil the Great explicitly references unwritten tradition as binding in De Spiritu Sancto.3 The canon of Scripture itself was determined through Tradition and conciliar authority — the very mechanisms that Sola Scriptura seeks to subordinate.
Rebuttal Patristic
Reply 8 replies
ReformedApologist Side A Tier II — Active 2 days ago
The canon argument is a common objection, but it confuses recognition with creation. The councils did not grant authority to Scripture; they recognized the authority already inherent in the inspired texts.4 Furthermore, Sola Scriptura does not reject all tradition — it subordinates tradition to the biblical text as the norming norm (norma normans).
Counter-Rebuttal Philosophical
Reply 3 replies
EcumenicalDove Neutral Tier II — Active 1 day ago
Both positions seem to presuppose a degree of historical continuity that warrants scrutiny. Might we consider a mediating view? Keith Mathison distinguishes Sola Scriptura (Scripture as the sole infallible authority, interpreted within the community) from Solo Scriptura (Scripture interpreted by the individual alone).5 This distinction may help bridge the apparent divide.
