Is Sola Scriptura a Valid Doctrine?

Side A: Sola Scriptura vs Side B: Scripture + Tradition

TheologianMark Side A Trusted Opening Argument 3 days ago

The principle of Sola Scriptura holds that Scripture alone is the final authority in all matters of faith and practice. This is grounded in passages such as 2 Timothy 3:16-17, where Paul writes that Scripture is "God-breathed" and sufficient to equip the believer for every good work. The early church fathers, while valuable, were not infallible -- and even they pointed back to Scripture as the ultimate standard.

PatristicReader Side B Rebuttal 2 days ago
The early church fathers... pointed back to Scripture as the ultimate standard.

This is a selective reading. Irenaeus and Tertullian explicitly argued that apostolic tradition was necessary to correctly interpret Scripture. The canon of Scripture itself was determined by Church councils -- so the authority of Scripture presupposes the authority of the Church that canonized it. You cannot have Sola Scriptura without first relying on Tradition to tell you what Scripture is.

ReformedApologist Side A Trusted Counter-Rebuttal 2 days ago

The recognition of the canon is not the same as creating authority. The Church recognized what was already authoritative -- it did not confer authority upon it. A shepherd recognizes his sheep; he does not make them sheep. The self-authenticating nature of Scripture is well-attested in Reformed epistemology (see Michael Kruger's work on canon formation).

EcumenicalBridge Neutral Clarifying Question 1 day ago

I find both sides compelling here. Could either party clarify: when we say "tradition," are we referring to Sacred Tradition (capital T) as defined by the Catholic Magisterium, or the broader concept of historical Christian consensus? This distinction seems important before the debate can progress further.
