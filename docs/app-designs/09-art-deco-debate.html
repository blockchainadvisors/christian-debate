TheologianMark Side A ♦ Trusted 3 days ago
The doctrine of Sola Scriptura, as articulated by the Reformers, holds that Scripture alone is the final authority for faith and practice. This does not deny the value of tradition, but subordinates it to the written Word.
"All Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness." — 2 Timothy 3:16
The early church fathers themselves appealed to Scripture as the highest authority when disputes arose.
Hermeneutics Reformation
PatristicaAnna Side B ♦♦ Scholar 2 days ago
With respect, this reading overlooks the fact that 2 Timothy was written before the New Testament canon was finalized. "Scripture" in Paul's context referred to the Old Testament. The canon itself was established through the authority of church councils — a tradition-based process.
Canon Formation Patristics
ReformedJoel Side A ♦ Trusted 2 days ago
The councils did not create the canon; they recognized what was already functioning as Scripture in the churches. Recognition is not the same as authority over the text. The Bereans in Acts 17:11 are praised precisely for testing teaching against Scripture.

Both sides raise valid historical points. I wonder if the real question is not whether tradition has authority, but what kind of authority it holds relative to Scripture. Even Protestant traditions have their own interpretive frameworks that function as de facto tradition.

Side A TheologianMark

Scripture presents itself as sufficient: "that the man of God may be complete, equipped for every good work" (2 Tim 3:17). If Scripture equips us for every good work, what necessity remains for tradition as a parallel authority?

Side B PatristicaAnna

Side A ReformedJoel
