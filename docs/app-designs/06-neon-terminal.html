> reformed_pastor ~ Side A Gold Systematic Theology
The doctrine of Sola Scriptura holds that Scripture alone is the final authority in matters of faith and practice. "All Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness" (2 Timothy 3:16). The early church fathers themselves pointed back to Scripture as the ultimate arbiter of truth, even when tradition was valued.
reply 3 days ago
> catholic_apologist ~ Side B Silver Church History
That passage doesn't say Scripture alone -- it says Scripture is profitable, not exclusive. Paul also writes "stand firm and hold to the traditions that you were taught by us, either by our spoken word or by our letter" (2 Thessalonians 2:15). Oral apostolic tradition is given equal weight here. The canon of Scripture itself was determined by the Church councils, which means Tradition necessarily preceded and authenticated Scripture.
reply 3 days ago
> berean_student ~ Side A Bronze Hermeneutics
The Bereans in Acts 17:11 tested Paul's own preaching against Scripture. If oral apostolic teaching was self-authenticating, why would Luke commend them for checking it against the written Word? This implies Scripture held a higher epistemic authority even during the apostolic era.
reply 2 days ago
> orthodox_inquirer ~ Neutral Silver Patristics
Both positions seem to have merit, but I wonder if the dichotomy is a false one. The Eastern Orthodox perspective holds that Scripture, Tradition, and the living experience of the Church form a unified whole -- you cannot truly have one without the others. Perhaps the real question is not "Scripture alone vs. Tradition" but "how do these sources of authority relate to each other?"
